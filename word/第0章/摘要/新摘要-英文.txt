Research and Construction of Ancient Tree Multimodal Hashing Retrieval System Based on Image and Geographic Metadata
Abstract
Ancient and famous trees represent invaluable carriers of natural ecology and historical culture, whose conservation and management hold significant implications for biodiversity maintenance and cultural heritage preservation. Currently, landscape and forestry management departments predominantly rely on manual inspections and paper-based documentation for the construction, retrieval, and management of ancient trees, resulting in critical issues including the fragmentation between image and geographic metadata, delayed data updates, and low-efficiency species identification. Owing to morphological similarities among certain ancient tree species and the fact that a substantial portion of records are stored solely in unstructured image formats or isolated geographic coordinates, traditional unimodal retrieval approaches fail to meet the rigorous demands of fine-grained management practices. Consequently, the construction of a multimodal retrieval system integrating image and geographic metadata, which achieves information complementarity through advanced multimodal fusion techniques, emerges as a pivotal breakthrough for enhancing the efficiency and efficacy of ancient tree conservation and management.This dissertation proposes a novel multimodal retrieval framework designated as DHLAM (Deep Hashing Learning Attention Multi-modal Network), which is grounded in deep hashing learning methodologies. By integrating multimodal fusion mechanisms into a unified deep hashing retrieval architecture, DHLAM achieves bidirectional retrieval capabilities encompassing image-to-location and location-to-image search functionalities. The primary contributions of this research are systematically articulated as follows:
(1)To address the challenges of class imbalance and complex background interference inherent in ancient tree multimodal retrieval, we propose the DHLAM model with a three-tier architecture. First, we formulate a joint loss function incorporating Triplet Loss and Contrastive Loss for metric learning optimization, complemented by hard negative mining strategies to mitigate class imbalance issues. Second, we construct a hash layer utilizing the FC-Tanh-Sign activation structure alongside a Quantization Loss module, which projects continuous feature representations into compact 128-bit binary hash codes, thereby substantially reducing storage overhead while accelerating retrieval speed. Third, we design a sophisticated multimodal attention fusion mechanism that constructs a unified semantic space through the joint embedding of image and geographic metadata, significantly enhancing the model's discriminative capability for critical ancient tree features.
(2)To comprehensively evaluate model performance, we establish a multi-dimensional experimental validation framework. We conduct comparative performance analyses between DHLAM and three state-of-the-art deep hashing baseline models, namely HashNet, DCH, and MDSH, to validate the architectural superiority of our proposed approach. Furthermore, we perform extensive evaluations using standard retrieval metrics including mAP@K, Recall@K, and Precision@K on the iNaturalist 2018 dataset and our self-constructed BFATH dataset to demonstrate model effectiveness. Finally, we execute comprehensive ablation studies to quantitatively verify the individual contributions of the hash layer, attention fusion mechanism, and joint loss function. Experimental results empirically demonstrate that DHLAM achieves superior performance over comparative methods in both retrieval speed and accuracy metrics.
(3) To address the critical gap in dedicated retrieval tools for ancient and famous trees, we design and implement a practical retrieval system based on B/S architecture. The system employs a decoupled front-end and back-end architecture utilizing Vue.js and SpringBoot frameworks, integrating the DHLAM model to support robust bidirectional retrieval of image and geographic metadata. The system has successfully completed development and rigorous testing phases, demonstrating stable and reliable operational performance.
Key Words: Ancient Tree Retrieval, Deep Hashing Learning, Multimodal Hashing Retrieval, DHLAM Model, Geographic Metadata, Attention Fusion Mechanism
