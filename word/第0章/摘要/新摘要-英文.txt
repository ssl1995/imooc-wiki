Research and Construction of Ancient Tree Multimodal Hashing Retrieval System Based on Image and Geographic Metadata

Abstract

Ancient and famous trees serve as vital carriers of natural ecology and historical culture, and their conservation and management are of significant importance for maintaining biodiversity and preserving cultural heritage. Currently, landscape and forestry management departments still predominantly rely on manual inspections and paper-based documentation for the construction, retrieval, and management of ancient trees, leading to issues such as fragmentation between image and geographic metadata, delayed information updates, and low efficiency in species identification. Due to the morphological similarities among certain ancient tree species and the fact that some records are stored solely in unstructured image formats or isolated location coordinates, traditional unimodal retrieval methods fail to meet the demands of practical fine-grained management. Therefore, constructing a multimodal retrieval system that fuses image and geographic metadata, and enables information complementarity through multimodal fusion techniques, has become a critical breakthrough for enhancing the efficiency of ancient tree conservation and management. This paper proposes DHLAM (Deep Hashing Learning Attention Multi-modal Network), a multimodal retrieval model based on deep hashing learning, which achieves image-to-location and location-to-image bidirectional retrieval capabilities by integrating multimodal fusion technology into a deep hashing retrieval framework. The main contributions of this work are as follows:

(1) To address the challenges of class imbalance and complex background interference in multimodal retrieval of ancient trees, we propose the DHLAM model. First, we design a joint loss function based on Triplet Loss and Contrastive Loss for metric learning optimization, and employ hard negative mining strategies to alleviate class imbalance. Second, we construct a hash layer (FC-Tanh-Sign) and quantization loss module to map continuous features into 128-bit binary hash codes, thereby reducing storage costs and accelerating retrieval speed. Finally, we design a multimodal attention fusion mechanism to construct a unified semantic space by combining image and geographic metadata, enhancing the model's discriminative capability for key features of ancient trees.

(2) To systematically evaluate model performance, we construct a multi-dimensional experimental validation framework. First, we compare DHLAM with three deep hashing baseline models including HashNet, DCH and MDSH to verify architectural superiority. Second, we conduct comparative evaluations using metrics of mAP@K, Recall@K, and Precision@K on iNaturalist 2018 dataset and our self-built BFATH dataset to validate model effectiveness. Finally, ablation experiments are performed to verify the contributions of the hash layer, attention fusion mechanism, and joint loss function. Experimental results demonstrate that DHLAM outperforms comparison methods in both retrieval speed and accuracy.

(3) To address the lack of dedicated retrieval tools for ancient and famous trees, we design and implement a B/S architecture-based retrieval system. The system adopts a front-end and back-end separated architecture using Vue and SpringBoot, integrating the DHLAM model to support bidirectional retrieval of image and geographic metadata. The system has completed development and testing, and operates stably.

Key Words: Ancient Tree Retrieval, Deep Hashing Learning, Multimodal Hashing Retrieval, DHLAM Model, Geographic Metadata
