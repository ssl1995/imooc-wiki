Research and Construction of Ancient and Famous Trees Retrieval System Based on Text-Image Multimodal Fusion
Abstract
Ancient and famous trees serve as vital carriers of natural ecology and historical culture, and their conservation and management are of significant importance for maintaining biodiversity and preserving cultural heritage. Currently, landscape and forestry management departments still predominantly rely on manual inspections and textual documentation for the construction, retrieval, and management of ancient trees, leading to issues such as fragmentation between image and text data, delayed information updates, and low efficiency in species identification. Due to the morphological similarities among certain ancient tree species and the fact that some records are stored solely in textual or unstructured image formats, traditional unimodal retrieval methods fail to meet the demands of practical fine-grained management. Therefore, constructing a multimodal retrieval system that fuses textual and visual information, and enables inter-modal information exchange through cross-modal techniques, has become a critical breakthrough for enhancing the efficiency of ancient tree conservation and management. This paper proposes CHNATR (Cross-modal Hashing Network for Ancient Tree Retrieval), a cross-modal retrieval model based on deep hashing learning, which achieves bidirectional retrieval capabilities of text-to-image and image-to-text by integrating cross-modal retrieval technology into a multimodal retrieval framework. The main contributions of this work are as follow:
(1)To address the challenges of class imbalance and complex background interference in cross-modal retrieval of ancient trees, we propose the CHNATR model. First, we design a joint loss function based on Triplet Loss and Contrastive Loss for metric learning optimization, and employ hard negative mining strategies to alleviate class imbalance. Second, we construct a hash layer (FC-Tanh-Sign) and quantization loss module to map continuous features into 128-bit binary hash codes, thereby reducing storage costs and accelerating retrieval speed. Finally, we design a cross-modal attention fusion mechanism to construct a unified semantic space by combining textual and visual information, enhancing the model's discriminative capability for key features of ancient trees.
(2)To systematically evaluate model performance, we construct a multi-dimensional experimental validation framework. First, we compare CHNATR with four cross-modal hashing baseline models including DCMH and PRDH to verify architectural superiority. Second, we conduct comparative evaluations using metrics of mAP@K, Recall@K, and Precision@K on datasets including iNaturalist 2018 and MSVD (with key frames extracted as image-text pairs) to validate model effectiveness. Finally, ablation experiments are performed to verify the contributions of the hash layer, attention fusion mechanism, and joint loss function. Experimental results demonstrate that CHNATR outperforms comparison methods in both retrieval speed and accuracy.
(3) To address the lack of dedicated retrieval tools for ancient and famous trees, we design and implement a B/S architecture-based retrieval system. The system adopts a front-end and back-end separated architecture using Vue and SpringBoot, integrating the CHNATR model to support bidirectional text-image retrieval. The system has completed development and testing, and operates stably.
Key Words: Ancient and Famous Tree Retrieval, Deep Hashing Learning,Cross-modal Retrieval,CHNATR Model
